    § 01

    The gap the review found

    Laid out end to end, the flow was complete in one direction and missing in the other. Everything from tap to release existed. Nothing existed for coming back.

    That matters more than it sounds, because the whole pitch of the pond is that it's somewhere your friends hang out. If the only thing a person can ever do is deposit a duck and leave, it isn't a place — it's a form. And practically: the private link was already being handed out at release with no screen behind it.

    So screens 09 and 10 are new. Coming back shows you your duck, how long it's been floating, and how many people waved. From there you can redecorate — straight into the same studio — or edit your message and contact.

    The one that isn't optional "Take my duck out." People leave a name, a message and sometimes a phone number on a stranger's website. There has to be a way to undo that which doesn't involve emailing you. Deleting the duck deletes its contact row in the same transaction — one action, everything gone, no "contact us to request removal".
    § 02

    What changed in the studio

    • Stickers know where they belong. Each of the 32 carries a slot — hat, face, neck, body, held, float — so "surprise me" puts hats on heads and bags at the duck's side instead of scattering things at random. The slot also gives keyboard users a sensible starting position.
    • They're draggable. Tap a placed sticker and drag; it snaps to the pixel grid as it moves. Tapping empty water places a new one.
    • Undo and Clear are always on screen, above the tabs, and they cover colour, stickers and paint together — one history, not three.
    • 32 stickers, including the handbags: top hat, cap, beanie, cowboy, crown, party, wizard, flower crown, halo, bow, sunglasses, glasses, eyepatch, blush, moustache, scarf, bow tie, chain, collar, heart, star, spot, stripes, pocket, balloon, fish, leaf, sparkle, handbag, tote, basket, satchel.
    • The pond strip shows your YOU tag — it was drawing off the top of the canvas before, so the one duck you were meant to be checking was the one without a label.

    Sign comes next, as you thought. Studio → Sign → Contact → Release. Decorating first is deliberate: by the time someone is asked for their name they've already spent four minutes making something, so the ask lands as signing your own work rather than filling in a form.
